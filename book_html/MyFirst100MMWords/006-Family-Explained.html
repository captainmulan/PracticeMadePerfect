One Roof, Many Smiles

Su woke in a wooden house while roosters called outside. Her mother stirred rice over the fire and hummed a soft tune. Her father swept the yard and waved to Grandmother, who was feeding the chickens. Grandfather sat on the porch with tea, watching the village wake up. Su ran to greet each elder with a smile, because in Myanmar the morning begins with the people who hold the family together.

Candles for Grandmother

When Thadingyut lights filled the village, Su's sister helped hang lanterns while her baby brother laughed at the glowing colors. Their aunt carried a tray of sweet rice cakes from the kitchen, and the whole house smelled of cinnamon and coconut. That evening Su bowed to Grandmother first and placed a candle in her hands. The baby clapped, the sister beamed, and even the aunt wiped happy tears — family love felt as warm as the flame.

Every Name Has a Place

After the feast, Uncle told jokes that made Cousin double over with laughter. Parents cleared the table while Su and Cousin practiced each family name aloud. Grandfather unrolled an old chart and pointed to every face — aunt, uncle, cousin, mother, father, and baby. By bedtime Su could name her whole Family without fear. She whispered a thank-you to her Parents and fell asleep proud of every branch on her tree.
