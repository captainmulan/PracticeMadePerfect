Chapter 6 · A Brave Voice in Class

To the Gate

Story

Cousin arrived early with two school bags and a determined smile. "Today you visit my class," Cousin said. Mudra's courage, which had grown at the tea shop and market, suddenly felt small again. School meant many eyes. School meant reading aloud.

They walked the dusty path together. At the gate, Teacher stood like a friendly lighthouse. "Good morning," Teacher said. Mudra swallowed. "Good morning, Teacher." The words came out clear enough to surprise her.

A Steady Line

Story

Inside the classroom, books waited in neat stacks. Pencils rested like tiny soldiers. Mudra sat beside Friend, who offered a shy wave. When Teacher asked Mudra to read one short line, the letters seemed to swim. Mudra's voice shook on the first word. Then it found the shore and grew steady.

Halfway through, Mudra's pencil tip broke with a soft snap. Panic rose. Friend slid another pencil across the desk without making a show of it. "Thank you," Mudra whispered. Teacher heard and nodded. "You are brave. Bravery is continuing."

Shared Pencil

Story

After the lesson, Mudra helped carry books to a shelf. She learned classroom, read, write, bag, pencil, friend — not as a list on a wall, but as tools she had needed in a real moment.

On the walk home Mudra told Grandma everything in a rush of English and Myanmar. Grandma listened fully. "A brave voice does not mean a loud voice," Grandma said. "It means a true one." Mudra touched her notebook. Challenge had not stopped her story. It had deepened it.
