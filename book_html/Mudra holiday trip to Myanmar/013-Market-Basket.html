Under Bright Umbrellas

Story

Grandma needed dinner. "Mudra, the market is loud and bright," she warned kindly. "Stay close. Use your finger and your manners." Mudra held the basket handle with both hands as if it were a steering wheel for courage.

Under colorful umbrellas, the market bloomed. Mangoes glowed like tiny suns. Tomatoes shone red. Eggs rested in neat rows. Silver fish lay on ice, eyes clear and fresh. Sellers called. Motorbikes hummed. Mudra's eyes darted everywhere, hungry to name what she saw.

I Want This One

Story

At the fish stall Mudra pointed. "I want this one." Grandma nodded. "Please," she reminded. Mudra added please, then thank you when the fish slid into a bag. At the next stall she chose eggs. At another, a firm red tomato. Each time her sentences grew less shaky.

A kind seller noticed Mudra's careful words and placed one small mango in her palm. "For the little traveler," he said. Mudra bowed her head. "Thank you." The mango smelled like sunshine and holidays.

Dinner We Chose

Story

On the way home the basket felt heavier, but Mudra felt lighter. Mother cooked the fish with rice. Mudra set the plates and poured water. Steam rose. Family gathered. "Tonight we eat what we chose together," Grandma said.

Mudra tasted a bite and whispered, "Delicious." In her notebook she wrote: Market day. I pointed. I asked. I thanked. The village is teaching me with food and kindness.
