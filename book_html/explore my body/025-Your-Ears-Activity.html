📖 Catch the vibration

Maya strums a rubber-band guitar — the band wiggles, air wiggles, and her eardrum wiggles too. Ears turn vibrations into sounds she recognises.
💡 Explanation: Sound is vibration travelling through air. The outer ear collects it; the eardrum and tiny bones pass it deeper inside.

📖 Three tiny bones

Maya sees a model of the hammer, anvil, and stirrup — the smallest bones in the body — passing vibrations like a relay team.
💡 Explanation: These ossicles amplify sound from the eardrum to the cochlea — a spiral organ filled with fluid and hair cells.

Picture · Balance buddies

📖 Balance buddies

Spinning on a chair, Maya feels dizzy when she stops. Her inner ear also senses balance and movement, not just sound.
💡 Explanation: Semicircular canals detect head rotation. Together with vision, they help you stay upright and coordinated.
