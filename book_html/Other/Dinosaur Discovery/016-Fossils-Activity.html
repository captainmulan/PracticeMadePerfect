Picture · Stone Memory

📖 Stone Memory

Kai chipped gently at gray rock until a rib appeared like a secret surfacing. "Fossils are time capsules," Dr. Mara said. "Minerals replaced bone cell by cell until the animal became stone — but the story remained."
💡 Explanation: Fossils are preserved traces of ancient life — bones, shells, tracks, eggs, and even footprints. Most form when remains are buried quickly and minerals replace organic material over millions of years.

Picture · Pages in the Cliff

📖 Pages in the Cliff

Layer upon layer of rock rose above the river — Triassic red, Jurassic gray, Cretaceous tan. Kai read the cliff like a book. "Deep equals old," Dr. Mara taught. "Each band is a chapter Earth wrote without ink."
💡 Explanation: Stratigraphy orders rock layers by age. Fossils in each layer show which organisms lived when. Older layers lie beneath younger ones in undisturbed rock.

Picture · Imprint of a Life

📖 Imprint of a Life

A fern left a ghost image in shale — no plant, just its shape. Kai mirrored it with his palm. "Not every fossil is bone," Dr. Mara said. "Sometimes life signs its name in negative space."
💡 Explanation: Body fossils are actual remains. Trace fossils are tracks, burrows, or coprolites (dung). Molds and casts capture external shapes. Each type reveals different behavior.
