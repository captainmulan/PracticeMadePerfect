Picture 1

📖 The Valley of Giants

Long before humans walked the Earth, a warm valley echoed with footsteps so heavy they shook the ferns. Young explorer Kai pressed his ear to a stone and swore he heard a heartbeat from another age. His grandmother laughed gently. "Those are not ghosts, Kai. Those are dinosaurs — rulers of a world we can only visit through stories and bones."

💡 What Makes a Dinosaur?

Dinosaurs were reptiles with legs held straight under their bodies — not sprawled like lizards. They lived on land during the Mesozoic Era. Not every big prehistoric creature was a dinosaur; sea reptiles and flying pterosaurs lived at the same time but were different groups.
Picture 2

📖 Bones That Speak

In a dusty museum basement, Kai found a femur taller than himself. A paleontologist named Dr. Mara showed him how the bone hollows and joint shapes revealed a fast runner. "Skeletons are diaries written in calcium," she said. Kai traced the rib cage with his finger and imagined lungs filling with prehistoric air.

💡 How We Know They Existed

Fossils — preserved remains in rock — prove dinosaurs were real. Bones, teeth, footprints, and even feather impressions tell us how they moved, ate, and looked. Scientists piece fossils together like a puzzle across continents.
Picture 3

📖 Sky Full of Cousins

At the aviary, Kai watched robins hop between branches and gasped. "Birds look like tiny dinosaurs!" Dr. Mara nodded. "Birds ARE dinosaurs — the only branch that survived. When you feed sparrows, you are caring for living relatives of T-Rex." Kai dropped breadcrumbs with new respect.

💡 Birds Are Living Dinosaurs

Modern birds evolved from small feathered theropods. They share hollow bones, wishbones, and egg-laying with their extinct cousins. When we study birds, we learn how many dinosaurs behaved and grew.
