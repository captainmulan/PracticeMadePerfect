Picture · One Supercontinent

📖 One Supercontinent

Kai spread continent-shaped puzzle pieces on the floor until they clicked into one giant land — Pangea. "No oceans between us and the neighbors," Dr. Mara said. In the Triassic, deserts stretched where seas would later form, and dust storms painted the sky copper.
💡 Explanation: All continents were joined as Pangea. The climate was generally hot and dry inland. Volcanoes erupted along rift zones as the supercontinent slowly began to break apart.

Picture · First Footprints

📖 First Footprints

A trail of three-toed prints crossed red stone like a poem. "These are among the earliest dinosaur tracks," Dr. Mara said. They belonged to small, quick hunters no bigger than dogs — the first chapter of the dinosaur story had begun.
💡 Explanation: Dinosaurs appeared around 230 million years ago. Early species like Coelophysis were slim bipeds. They competed with other reptiles but would eventually dominate as competitors faded.

Picture · Desert Survivors

📖 Desert Survivors

Kai sipped water sparingly on a field trip, mirroring Triassic life. Tough plants with deep roots fed early plant-eaters while mammal-like cynodonts hid in burrows. Life was sparse but resilient — a rehearsal for bigger dramas to come.
💡 Explanation: Triassic ecosystems included early dinosaurs, mammal relatives, crocodile relatives, and giant amphibians. Dinosaurs were not yet the rulers — they shared the stage until a mass extinction cleared the way.
