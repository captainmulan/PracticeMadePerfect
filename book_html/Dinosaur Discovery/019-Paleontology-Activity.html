📖 First Brush Stroke

Sun baked the dig site as Kai swept dust from a jaw fragment. "Patience," Dr. Mara called. "We remove millimeters, not shovels." The bone emerged like a smile from the past.
💡 Explanation: Paleontologists survey sites, map locations, and excavate with hand tools. Bones are jacketed in plaster for transport. Every piece is labeled with precise coordinates.

📖 Tools of Time Travel

Brushes, picks, GPS, field notebooks — Kai packed like an explorer. "Technology helps," Dr. Mara said, "but careful eyes find what machines miss."
💡 Explanation: Scientists use geological maps, aerial surveys, and CT scans. Field notes record rock type, orientation, and associated fossils. Context is as important as the bone itself.

Picture · Lab of Questions

📖 Lab of Questions

Under fluorescent lights, fossils were cleaned with tiny pneumatic tools. Kai watched a skull appear from matrix like a moon from clouds. "The lab is where bones become answers — and new questions."
💡 Explanation: Fossil prep removes rock without damaging specimen. Microscopes and scanners reveal growth rings, injuries, and pathology. Isotope analysis can hint at diet and climate.
