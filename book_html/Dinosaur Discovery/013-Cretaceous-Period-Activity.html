Picture · World in Bloom

📖 World in Bloom

Flowers appeared — and with them, new partnerships between plants and insects. Kai sniffed magnolia-like blooms in the botanic dome. "The Cretaceous smelled sweeter," Dr. Mara said, "and predators grew cleverer."
💡 Explanation: Angiosperms (flowering plants) spread during the Cretaceous. New foods supported diverse herbivores and changed forest structure worldwide.

Picture · King of the Forest

📖 King of the Forest

T-Rex roared from a mural, six tons of attitude. Kai measured a tooth — banana-sized. "T-Rex had bone-crushing bite force," Dr. Mara said. "But it also had patience — like a vulture with pride."
💡 Explanation: T-Rex lived near the end of the Cretaceous in North America. It had excellent binocular vision, powerful legs, and could scavenge as well as hunt.

Picture · Three Horns, One Family

📖 Three Horns, One Family

A Triceratops herd crossed Kai's video game screen — babies in the center, adults on the rim. "Horns and frills were signals and shields," Dr. Mara explained. Unity was their superpower.
💡 Explanation: Ceratopsians were four-legged plant-eaters with frilled heads and horns. Triceratops used its frill for display and protection. They lived in groups for safety.
