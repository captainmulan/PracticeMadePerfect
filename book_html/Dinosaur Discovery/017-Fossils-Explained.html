🦴 Fossils — Explained

Picture · story · explanation × 3
Picture 1

📖 Insect in Gold

Amber held a mosquito like a jewel. Kai peered at legs finer than thread. "Sap trapped creatures mid-step," Dr. Mara whispered. "Windows to forest life that bone alone can't show."

💡 Preservation in Amber

Tree resin can encase small organisms and harden into amber. Soft tissues and details survive far better than in typical rock fossils. DNA is usually too degraded for cloning.
Picture 2

📖 Steps Across Mud

A trail of prints crossed the exhibit floor — heavy sauropod, light raptor, dragging tail mark. Kai hopscotched beside them. "Tracks show speed, direction, and who walked together," Dr. Mara said.

💡 Trace Fossils

Footprints fossilize when mud or sand hardens around a step. Trackways reveal herd behavior, running speed, and ancient environments. They are as valuable as skeletons.
Picture 3

📖 Hall of Rebuilt Giants

Mounted skeletons towered over Kai — steel armatures hidden inside ribs. "Museums assemble puzzles missing pieces," Dr. Mara said. "Science and art shake hands here."

💡 From Fossil to Display

Paleontologists excavate, jacket, and prepare fossils in labs. Missing bones may be modeled from related species. Mounts educate the public and test hypotheses about posture.
